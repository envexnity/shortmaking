# The Great Emu War (1932) — 3D Kaz-style vertical short

## Facts (sources: Wikipedia "Emu War"; Australian War Memorial; National Archives of Australia)
- Nov–Dec 1932, Campion district, Western Australia. ~20,000 emus raided wheat farms after breeding season.
- Major G.P.W. Meredith (7th Heavy Battery, Royal Australian Artillery), two soldiers, two Lewis guns, 10,000 rounds.
- 2 Nov: first encounter — emus ran out of range of the guns.
- 4 Nov: ambush at a local dam, 1,000+ emus approach; gun jammed after ~12 killed.
- Gun mounted on a truck: truck could not keep up, ground too rough to fire. Emus run up to ~50 km/h.
- Meredith: each flock had "its own leader ... a big black-plumed bird ... keeping watch".
- Final claim by 10 Dec: 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (≈10 per bird).
- Meredith: emus "can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks."
- Emu on Australia's coat of arms since 1908.

1. In 1932, Australia sent soldiers with machine guns into a war against birds. And lost.
2. Twenty thousand emus had stormed the wheat fields of Western Australia.
3. So the army sent Major Meredith, two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets.
4. On day one, the emus simply ran out of range.
5. Two days later, he ambushed a thousand of them at a dam.
6. The gun jammed after twelve.
7. Luckily, he had a new plan: mount the gun on a truck.
8. Unluckily, emus can run fifty kilometres an hour, over ground no truck could handle.
9. You see, every flock posted a tall lookout.
10. The moment the guns appeared, they scattered in every direction, like trying to catch a bag of spilled marbles.
11. By December, the army claimed nine hundred and eighty-six birds, at roughly ten bullets each.
12. Meredith admitted the emus could face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks.
13. The soldiers went home. The emus stayed.
14. And today, the emu is on Australia's coat of arms. Undefeated.
