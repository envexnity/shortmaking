# What Would Kill You First On Mars? — Script & Scene Plan (Orbit 2D Style)

- **Channel**: **Orbit Oddities** (`@OrbitOddities`)
- **Format**: Orbit 2D Space Survival Explainer (`1920×1080`, `30 fps`)
- **Pacing Target**: Calm, deadpan narrator (`~2.6 wps`, `~95 seconds`, `15` scenes)
- **Script Rules Checked**:
  1. **Opens on a rule, then breaks it**: Beat 01 establishes Mars as a calm cold desert ("deserts don't kill astronauts — that's the rule"), then Beat 02 breaks it immediately ("Then your tongue starts to fizz.").
  2. **Every number gets a comparison**: `6 mbar` (<1% of Earth's `1,013 mbar`, water boils at `37°C` body heat); `-60°C / -125°C` (colder than any night ever recorded in Antarctica); `0.64 mSv/day` (`6 chest X-rays` a day); `90 days` vs `14.4 years`; `22 minutes` one-way light delay.
  3. **A dry joke + 1 visual gag after every scary fact**: `"MARS SODA CO."` receipt (`$0`, `TOTAL: YOU`, `"CARBONATED"` stamp) and `"DOORMAT NOTICE: WIPE FEET please"`.
  4. **"What can you do?" — every option knocked down**: Holding breath, digging into frozen perchlorate soil, calling Earth (`22 min` one-way delay), and an explicit `UNPROVEN` crust magma twist.

## Beats

1. Picture this. You're standing on Mars, fourth planet from the Sun. Looking around, it's just a cold red desert — and deserts don't kill astronauts. That's the rule.
2. Then your tongue starts to fizz. What actually kills you first on Mars?
3. Killer number one: the thin air.
4. Mars has an atmospheric pressure of just six millibars, compared to one thousand and thirteen on Earth. That is under one percent of Earth's air. At six millibars, water boils at thirty-seven degrees Celsius — your exact body temperature.
5. Remember that fizz? Before you even suffocate, the saliva on your tongue and the moisture in your eyes literally start to boil — like a warm soda you never ordered.
6. Let's say we patch your helmet seal. Killer number two: the cold.
7. Because Martian air is too thin to trap heat, the average surface temperature is minus sixty degrees Celsius. Near the poles in winter, it plunges to minus one hundred and twenty-five — colder than any night ever recorded in Antarctica.
8. Give your suit a heater, and you walk straight into killer number three: the toxic dust.
9. In two thousand and eight, NASA's Phoenix Lander scooped up Martian dirt and confirmed it is packed with perchlorates — toxic salts that wreck your thyroid, plus talcum-fine grains that cling to your boots and scar your lungs if you track them inside. Wipe your feet, please.
10. Seal the airlock and scrub the suit, and killer number four is already hitting you: radiation.
11. Mars has no global magnetic field. Every single day on the surface blasts you with zero point six four millisieverts of cosmic radiation — roughly the dose of six chest X-rays every twenty-four hours.
12. Robots handle the radiation better than we do, until the dust settles on their solar panels. NASA's Opportunity rover was designed to last ninety days, yet survived fourteen point four years before a planet-wide dust storm turned out the lights.
13. So what can you do? Hold your breath? Your lungs rupture in seconds. Dig underground? The dirt is frozen rock laced with perchlorates. Call Earth for help? At Mars's farthest orbit, your radio message takes twenty-two minutes just to travel one way.
14. Even burying miles down might not guarantee safety — seismic readings near Alba Mons hint liquid magma could still pool eight kilometers below the surface, though for now that remains unproven.
15. On Mars, the desert doesn't kill you. Everything else just gets in line first.
