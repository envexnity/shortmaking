On Friday, April 13, 2029, the 1,100-foot asteroid Apophis will pass closer to Earth (31,600 km) than our geostationary TV and weather satellites (35,786 km).

Movies say if an asteroid aims at Earth, we just launch a nuclear warhead. Here is why that plan actually kills us faster:
1. The Shotgun Effect: NASA's OSIRIS-REx mission proved most asteroids aren't solid iron — they're loose flying piles of rubble. Blowing one up late turns 1 cannonball into 10,000 radioactive shotgun pellets still moving at 20 km/s.
2. The 400°C Sky Broiler: Billions of rock fragments entering the atmosphere at once superheat the sky until forests ignite globally.
3. The Real Fix (NASA DART): Instead of blowing it up late, you ram it years early at 14,000 mph — because a 2 cm/s push years in advance makes it miss Earth completely.
