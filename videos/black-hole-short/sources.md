# Science & NASA/JPL Sources — What Actually Happens If You Fall Into a Black Hole?

## 1. Verified NASA / Relativistic Astrophysics Facts

| Claim in Video | Verified Value | Primary Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tidal Gravity Differential ("Spaghettification")** | Around a stellar-mass black hole (`~10 solar masses`, Schwarzschild radius `~30 km`), tidal acceleration scales as $\Delta g \approx \frac{2GM}{r^3} \Delta r$. At `~300 km` altitude, the vertical stretching + horizontal compression across a `2 m` astronaut reaches millions of $g$'s. | NASA Goddard Space Flight Center Black Hole Guide; Kip Thorne, *Black Holes and Time Warps* |
| **Gravitational Time Dilation & Redshift** | As an infalling observer approaches the Schwarzschild radius $r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$, clock rate relative to a distant observer slows by $\sqrt{1 - r_s/r}$ and outgoing photons redshift toward infinity — making the astronaut appear to freeze and turn deep red to outside observers. | Einstein General Relativity (Schwarzschild Metric) / NASA JPL Black Hole Education |
| **Inside the Event Horizon (River Model of Space)** | Inside $r < r_s$, the radial coordinate becomes timelike; space itself flows inward faster than $c = 300,000\text{ km/s}$, making outward escape physically impossible regardless of thrust. | Hamilton & Lisle (2008), *American Journal of Physics* / NASA Goddard |
| **Supermassive Black Hole M87\* Horizon Scale** | **M87\*** has a mass of `6.5 billion suns` (`± 0.7 billion`) and an event horizon diameter of `~38 billion km` (`~254 AU`, over 3× Pluto's average orbital diameter of `~11.8 billion km`). Because tidal forces at the horizon scale as $1/M^2$, tidal stress at M87\*'s horizon is `< 1 g` (imperceptible). | Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration (2019), *Astrophysical Journal Letters* / NASA JPL (`PIA23122`) |
